#+title: LispM Kit

#+HTML: <p align="center"><a href="https://github.com/LispMachineLab/lispm-kit/actions/workflows/test.yml"><img src="https://github.com/LispMachineLab/lispm-kit/actions/workflows/test.yml/badge.svg" alt="test" /></a></p>

What LispM and the applications of its App Store are made with, in any Emacs 30.1 or later: an
application's commands, each declared once, and a program's words, in the language spoken.  It
knows of no interface: Emacs's own shows what an application declares with it as it shows any
mode's, and [[https://github.com/LispMachineLab/lispm-desktop][LispM Desktop]] reads the same declarations from the mode's symbol, for its buttons and
its window of keys.  In code, the prefix is ~lispm-kit-~.

* An application's commands, each declared once

An application's mode used to say what a command is three or four times over: its key in the
keymap, its item in an ~easy-menu-define~, and again in whatever interface showed it as a button
or listed its key.  Each copy could drift from the others.  ~lispm-kit-define-commands~ says it
once:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(require 'lispm-kit-commands)

(lispm-kit-define-commands lispm-rotom-mode
  :menu "Rotom"
  (lispm-rotom-start-or-stop
   :key "s" :help "Start or stop the daemon"
   :label (if (lispm-rotom-running-p) "Stop Daemon" "Start Daemon")
   :button (if (lispm-rotom-running-p) "Stop" "Start")
   :enabled (and (lispm-rotom-installed-p) (not lispm-rotom-busy)))
  (lispm-rotom-restart
   :key "r" :help "Restart the daemon" :label "Restart Daemon" :button "Restart"
   :enabled (and (lispm-rotom-running-p) (not lispm-rotom-busy)))
  "---"
  (lispm-rotom-reload :key "g" :help "Read Rotom again" :label "Read Again"))
#+end_src

A command's properties:

| ~:key~      | a key, or a list of them, as ~keymap-set~ takes them                                |
| ~:label~    | the words of its menu item, a string or a form returning one; without it, no item |
| ~:help~     | what it does, in a few words                                                       |
| ~:enabled~  | a form, non-nil while it can be used                                               |
| ~:button~   | makes it one of the main commands: the words of its button, or ~t~ for ~:label~'s |
| ~:style~    | ~toggle~ or ~radio~, for an item checked or not                                    |
| ~:selected~ | a form, non-nil while its item is checked                                          |

Any form may be a function instead, ~(lambda () ...)~ or ~#'NAME~; all are evaluated in the mode's
buffer.  ~:menu TITLE~ starts a menu of the menu bar, holding what follows up to the next one;
commands before the first are in no menu.  The menus are made one after another as
~easy-menu-define~ makes them, so the menu bar shows the later ones first.  Besides commands, a
menu holds ~"---"~ separators, submenus ~(TITLE ITEM ...)~ (easy-menu's keywords, as ~:filter~,
may follow the title), a command declared elsewhere in the form, named alone, to put its item
there rather than where it is declared, which keeps its place among the buttons, and items as
~easy-menu-define~ takes them, ~[...]~, as they are.

The keys are bound in ~MODE-map~, defined if it is not yet, and the menus are its menu bar's:
plain Emacs shows them, and =C-h m= lists the keys.  What was declared is MODE's
~lispm-kit-commands~ property, a list of property lists in the order declared, one a command:
~:command~, ~:key~ (a list), ~:label~, ~:help~, ~:enabled~, ~:button~, ~:style~, ~:selected~,
the forms made functions of no arguments and ~:button t~ made the words of ~:label~.  An
interface needs nothing of this library to read it:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(seq-filter (lambda (command) (plist-get command :button))
            (get 'lispm-rotom-mode 'lispm-kit-commands))
#+end_src

The words are in English; an interface may translate them where it shows them.

* A program's words, in the language spoken

A program's words are written in English in its code, and shown in English, German, French,
Japanese or Chinese, Simplified or Traditional, from catalogs of its own, one a language.  What
finds the language, reads the catalogs and translates is ~lispm-kit-i18n~, once for every
program: LispM Agent, LispM Desktop and LispM UI each keep only their catalogs.  A program says
once where they are and how it chooses its language, as a domain, a symbol naming its words:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(require 'lispm-kit-i18n)

(defcustom lispm-mail-language nil
  "The language LispM Mail speaks, or nil for the program around's, else the computer's."
  :type 'symbol)
(defvar lispm-mail-language-functions nil)
(defvar lispm-mail-language-changed-hook nil)

(lispm-kit-i18n-define 'lispm-mail
  :directory (expand-file-name "i18n" lispm-mail-directory)
  :language 'lispm-mail-language
  :language-functions 'lispm-mail-language-functions
  :changed-hook 'lispm-mail-language-changed-hook)

(defun lispm-mail-tr (message &rest args)
  (apply #'lispm-kit-tr 'lispm-mail message args))
#+end_src

The domain speaks the language its ~:language~ variable names, else that of the program it is
shown by, which a function of its ~:language-functions~ returns, else the computer's own; once
that changes, its ~:changed-hook~ runs, to draw again what shows words.  Every word a person
reads passes through ~lispm-kit-tr~, ~lispm-kit-tr-n~ for a count, ~lispm-kit-tr-in~ for words
meant in a context, or ~lispm-kit-format-time~; words kept as a file loads are marked with
~lispm-kit-tr-noop~ and translated where shown.  What models read stays English, in
~lispm-kit-in-english~.

The catalogs are the files of the domain's ~:directory~, one per language, named by its tag, as
=fr.eld=:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(:name "Français"
 :messages (("Open" . "Ouvrir")
            (("state" . "Open") . "Ouvert")
            ("%d message" . ("%d message" "%d messages"))))
#+end_src

=lispm-kit-i18n-check.el= is for a program's tests: ~lispm-kit-i18n-messages~ finds the
messages its code gives to translate, and ~lispm-kit-i18n-problems~ says what its catalogs lack
or hold no longer, ~lispm-kit-i18n-report~ printing it for =make i18n=.

* Installing it

LispM's App Store installs it before the applications that need it.  Elsewhere, from git:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(package-vc-install "https://github.com/LispMachineLab/lispm-kit")
#+end_src

A program names it in its =Package-Requires=, as ~(lispm-kit "0.2.0")~, and requires the part it
uses, ~lispm-kit-commands~ or ~lispm-kit-i18n~, or ~lispm-kit~ for both.
